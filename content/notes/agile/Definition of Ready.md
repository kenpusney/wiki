
The [[Definition of Ready]] (DoR) is a set of criteria that a product backlog item must meet before the team can commit to it in a sprint planning meeting. It's the complement to the [[Definition of Done]] (DoD).

While the DoD describes when work is *complete*, the DoR describes when a backlog item is *ready to be worked on*.

## What is the Definition of Ready?

The Definition of Ready ensures that the team doesn't commit to work that is poorly understood, incomplete, or blocked. It prevents the common problem of starting a sprint with half-baked stories that lead to confusion, rework, and missed sprint goals.

## Common Definition of Ready checklist

### Story quality
- [ ] User story follows the standard format: "As a [user], I want [action], so that [benefit]"
- [ ] [[Acceptance Criteria]] are defined and agreed upon
- [ ] Story is small enough to complete within a single sprint
- [ ] Story is estimated with [[User Story Points|story points]]

### Dependencies and blockers
- [ ] Dependencies on other teams or systems are identified
- [ ] No external blockers prevent work from starting
- [ ] Required environments and tools are available

### Design and UX
- [ ] UX/UI designs are available (if applicable)
- [ ] Design has been reviewed and approved
- [ ] Accessibility requirements are documented

### Technical readiness
- [ ] Technical approach has been discussed with the team
- [ ] Architecture decisions have been made
- [ ] API contracts are defined (if applicable)

### Business readiness
- [ ] Business value is clearly articulated
- [ ] Stakeholder approval has been obtained
- [ ] Regulatory or compliance requirements are documented

## Definition of Ready vs Definition of Done

| Aspect | [[Definition of Ready]] | [[Definition of Done]] |
|--------|------------------------|----------------------|
| **When applied** | Before sprint planning | During/after sprint execution |
| **Purpose** | Ensure item is ready to start | Ensure work is complete |
| **Scope** | Individual backlog items | All work items |
| **Owner** | Product Owner + Team | Entire Scrum Team |

## Tips for creating a Definition of Ready

1. **Keep it practical** — A DoR that's too strict will block all progress; one that's too loose will let poorly defined work through
2. **Involve the whole team** — The DoR is a team commitment, not just the Product Owner's responsibility
3. **Review and evolve** — As the team matures, refine the DoR based on what you've learned
4. **Don't make it a gatekeeper** — The DoR is a guide, not a rigid checklist that prevents all flexibility

## How the DoR connects to other practices

- [[Backlog Refinement]] — The DoR should be applied during refinement to ensure items are ready before sprint planning
- [[Sprint Planning]] — The DoR helps the team confidently commit to items during planning
- [[User Stories]] — The DoR ensures stories have proper acceptance criteria and are well-defined
- [[User Story Points]] — Story point estimation is often part of the DoR checklist
