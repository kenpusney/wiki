
An Increment is a concrete stepping stone toward the [[Sprint Goal]]. Each Increment is a sum of all the [[Definition of Done|completed]] [[Backlog Prioritization|Product Backlog items]].

## What is an Increment?

In Scrum, an increment is the sum of all the Product Backlog items completed during a Sprint combined with the value of the increments of all previous Sprints. At the end of a Sprint, the new Increment must be:

- **Usable** — It must work and be tested
- **Complete** — It must meet the [[Definition of Done]]
- **Integrated** — It must be merged with all previous work

## Key Characteristics

### Must meet Definition of Done
An increment that doesn't meet the [[Definition of Done]] cannot be released. It's not "done" even if the Product Owner says so.

### Must be usable
The increment should be in a usable condition, regardless of whether the Product Owner decides to release it.

### Must be integrated
The increment must be integrated with all previous work. It's not a separate branch or feature — it's part of the product.

## Increment vs Release

| Aspect | Increment | Release |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| **When created** | End of each Sprint | When the organization decides |
| **Scope** | Sum of completed work | Subset of increments |
| **Decision** | Scrum Team | Organization/Product Owner |
| **Frequency** | Every Sprint | As needed |

An organization may choose to release after every Sprint, or accumulate several Sprints' worth of increments before releasing.

## Increment and Scrum Artifacts

The Increment is closely related to other Scrum artifacts:

- **Product Backlog** — The source of work that becomes increments
- **Sprint Backlog** — The work planned for the current Sprint to create an increment
- **Definition of Done** — The quality standard that increments must meet

## Increment and Scrum Events

- **[[Sprint Planning]]** — Plans the work to create the increment
- **[[Daily Scrum]]** — Tracks progress toward creating the increment
- **[[Sprint Review]]** — Inspects the increment with stakeholders
- **[[Retrospectives]]** — Improves how the team creates increments

## Common Misunderstandings

### "An increment is a release"
Not necessarily. An increment is ready for release, but the organization may choose to release multiple increments together.

### "An increment must be perfect"
An increment must meet the [[Definition of Done]], but it doesn't have to be feature-complete. It should be a usable, tested, integrated increment.

### "The Product Owner decides when to release"
The Product Owner decides what's in the Product Backlog, but the release decision may involve other stakeholders or organizational processes.
