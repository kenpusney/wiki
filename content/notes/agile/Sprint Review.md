
The Sprint Review is one of the four [[Agile Ceremonies|Scrum ceremonies]], held at the end of each sprint. It's the time to showcase the team's completed work and gather feedback from stakeholders.

## What is a Sprint Review?

The Sprint Review is an inspect-and-adapt event where the Scrum Team presents the results of the Sprint to key stakeholders. The purpose is to gather feedback on the product increment and ensure the product is on the right track.

Unlike the [[Retrospectives|Sprint Retrospective]], which focuses on *how* the team worked, the Sprint Review focuses on *what* was built.

## Who attends?

- **Required**: Development Team, [[Scrum Master|Scrum Master]], Product Owner
- **Optional**: Management, customers, developers from other teams, and other stakeholders

Stakeholder attendance is highly valuable because their feedback directly informs the next sprint's priorities.

## What happens during a Sprint Review?

### 1. The Product Owner explains what was completed
The Product Owner discusses which [[Backlog Prioritization|product backlog items]] were completed during the Sprint and which were not.

### 2. The Development Team demonstrates the work
The team showcases the working product increment. Focus on:
- Product functionality
- User experience
- Customer value
- Business value delivered

### 3. Stakeholders provide feedback
This is the core value of the Sprint Review. Stakeholders:
- Ask questions and provide feedback
- Discuss what they've seen
- Collaborate on what should be done next
- Re-prioritize the [[Sprint Backlog|product backlog]] based on what was learned

### 4. Review the timeline and adapt
The group reviews:
- Budget and timeline projections
- Potential capabilities for the next release
- Adaptations to the product backlog based on feedback

## Sprint Review timing

| Sprint length | Recommended review duration |
|---------------|---------------------------|
| 1 week        | 1 hour                    |
| 2 weeks       | 2 hours                   |
| 4 weeks       | 4 hours                   |

A general guideline: 1 hour per week of sprint length.

## Sprint Review vs Sprint Retrospective

Many teams confuse these two ceremonies. They serve very different purposes:

| Aspect | Sprint Review | [[Retrospectives|Sprint Retrospective]] |
|--------|--------------|--------------------------------------|
| **Focus** | Product — *what* was built | Process — *how* the team worked |
| **Audience** | Includes external stakeholders | Internal Scrum Team only |
| **Output** | Updated product backlog | Action items for process improvement |
| **Tone** | Demonstration and feedback | Reflection and adaptation |

## Common mistakes to avoid

1. **Skipping the review** — Never skip the Sprint Review, even if the sprint didn't go well. Stakeholders need to see progress and provide feedback.
2. **Showcasing incomplete work** — Only demonstrate work that meets the team's [[Definition of Done]]. Incomplete work creates false expectations.
3. **Turning it into a status meeting** — The review is about the product, not individual performance. Avoid making it a report-to-management session.
4. **Ignoring stakeholder feedback** — Collecting feedback and then ignoring it erodes trust. Act on what you hear.
5. **No preparation** — Rehearse the demo. Technical difficulties during a live demo are distracting and unprofessional.

## Tips for effective Sprint Reviews

- **Focus on business value** — Don't just show features; explain the value they deliver to customers
- **Make it interactive** — Encourage questions and discussion throughout the demo
- **Keep it visual** — Use the actual product, not slides
- **Celebrate wins** — Acknowledge the team's effort and accomplishments
- **Document feedback** — Capture stakeholder input for the next [[Sprint Planning|sprint planning]] session
- **Time-box strictly** — Respect the time limit to keep energy high
