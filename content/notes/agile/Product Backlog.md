
The Product Backlog is an ordered list of everything that might be needed in the product, and the single source of work for the Scrum Team. It is the [[Scrum Framework|Scrum]] artifact that emerges from [[Sprint Planning|Sprint Planning]].

## What is the Product Backlog?

The Product Backlog is a living document. It's never complete. The earliest development of it only lays out the initially known and best-understood requirements. The Product Backlog evolves as the product and the environment in which it will be used evolve.

### Key characteristics

- **Ordered** — Highest value items at the top
- **Emergent** — Evolves as understanding grows
- **Living** — Never complete, always refined
- **Single source** — The only source of work for the Scrum Team

## Product Backlog Items

A Product Backlog item (PBI) typically includes:

- **Description** — What the item is
- **Order/Priority** — How important it is
- **Estimate** — How much effort it will take ([[User Story Points|story points]])
- **Value** — How much value it delivers
- **[[Acceptance Criteria]]** — What "done" looks like

### Types of Product Backlog items

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **User Stories** | Requirements from the user's perspective |
| **Bug Fixes** | Issues that need to be resolved |
| **Technical Work** | Infrastructure, refactoring, etc. |
| **Knowledge Acquisition** | Research, investigation, [[Spike]] |

## Product Backlog Refinement

The Product Backlog is continuously refined through [[Backlog Refinement]]:

- Adding new items
- Reprioritizing existing items
- Estimating effort
- Splitting large items
- Removing obsolete items

## Product Backlog vs [[Sprint Backlog]]

| Aspect | Product Backlog | [[Sprint Backlog]] |
|--------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Scope** | Everything the product might need | Work for the current Sprint |
| **Owner** | Product Owner | Development Team |
| **Duration** | Lives as long as the product | Dies at the end of the Sprint |
| **Content** | All items, prioritized | Selected items + plan |
| **Changes** | Continuous | Only by the team during Sprint |

## The Product Backlog is a Conversation

The Product Backlog is more than a list — it's a shared understanding between the Product Owner and the Development Team. It represents:

- **What** the product needs
- **Why** each item matters
- **How much** effort each item will take
- **When** each item should be delivered

## Product Backlog and Scrum Events

- **[[Sprint Planning]]** — Items are selected from the Product Backlog
- **[[Daily Scrum]]** — Team discusses progress, may identify new items
- **[[Sprint Review]]** — Stakeholders provide feedback that may add new items
- **[[Retrospectives]]** — Team may identify improvements to add to the Product Backlog
- **[[Backlog Refinement]]** — Continuous activity to keep the Product Backlog healthy

## Product Backlog and [[Definition of Ready]]

Items in the Product Backlog should meet the [[Definition of Ready]] before being selected for a Sprint. This ensures:
- The item is well understood
- The item can be completed in a Sprint
- The item delivers value
- The item is estimated

## Product Backlog Metrics

Track these metrics to understand Product Backlog health:

- **Item count** — How many items are in the Product Backlog?
- **Age** — How long have items been sitting in the Product Backlog?
- **Priority distribution** — Is the Product Backlog properly prioritized?
- **Completion rate** — How many items are being completed per Sprint?
