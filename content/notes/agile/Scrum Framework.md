
Scrum is a lightweight framework that helps people, teams and organizations generate value through adaptive solutions for complex problems.

## Scrum Framework Overview

Scrum is built on the empiricism of lean thinking and the three pillars:
- **Transparency** — Everyone involved must be able to see what's happening
- **Inspection** — The Scrum artifacts must be regularly checked for problems
- **Adaptation** — Adjust when something is not meeting quality standards

## The Three Pillars

### Transparency
Everyone involved must be able to see what's happening:
- [[Sprint Backlog]] is visible to all
- [[Definition of Done]] is shared and understood
- Progress is visible through [[Burndown and Burnup Charts|burndown charts]]
- [[Daily Scrum]] keeps everyone informed

### Inspection
The Scrum artifacts must be regularly checked for problems:
- [[Sprint Review]] inspects the [[Increment]]
- [[Retrospectives]] inspects the process
- [[Daily Scrum]] inspects progress toward the [[Sprint Goal]]
- [[Definition of Done]] ensures quality standards

### Adaptation
Adjust when something is not meeting quality standards:
- [[Sprint Planning]] adapts the plan based on new information
- [[Retrospectives]] identifies improvements
- [[Sprint Goal]] can be adapted (within the Sprint)
- [[Product Backlog]] is continuously refined

## Scrum Theory

Scrum is founded on empiricism and lean thinking. It employs an iterative, incremental approach to optimize predictability and control risk.

### Empiricism
Knowledge comes from experience, and decisions are based on observation, not detailed upfront planning.

### Lean Thinking
Focus on the essentials, eliminate waste, and continuously improve.

## Scrum Values

The Scrum Values create the culture that makes Scrum work:
- **[[Scrum Values#1. Commitment|Commitment]]** — Commit to goals
- **[[Scrum Values#2. Focus|Focus]]** — Focus on the Sprint
- **[[Scrum Values#3. Openness|Openness]]** — Be open about work and challenges
- **[[Scrum Values#4. Respect|Respect]]** — Respect each other
- **[[Scrum Values#5. Courage|Courage]]** — Have courage to do the right thing

## Scrum Team

The Scrum Team is a small, cross-functional, self-managing team:

### Product Owner
- Defines [[Sprint Goal|what]] to build
- Manages the [[Backlog Prioritization|Product Backlog]]
- Maximizes value

### Scrum Master
- Defines [[Sprint Goal|how]] to work
- Facilitates Scrum events
- Removes impediments

### Developers
- Creates the [[Increment]]
- Self-organizes around the work
- Cross-functional skills

## Scrum Events

Scrum uses time-boxed events (also called ceremonies):

| Event               | Purpose                                 | Duration         |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| [[Sprint]]          | Create a valuable, useful Increment     | 1-4 weeks        |
| [[Sprint Planning]] | Plan the work for the Sprint            | 1-2 hours/week   |
| [[Daily Scrum]]     | Inspect progress toward Sprint Goal     | 15 minutes       |
| [[Sprint Review]]   | Inspect the Increment with stakeholders | 1 hour/week      |
| [[Retrospectives]]  | Improve the process                     | 1-1.5 hours/week |

## Scrum Artifacts

Scrum artifacts represent work or value:

| Artifact            | Description                                         |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| [[Product Backlog]] | Ordered list of everything needed in the product    |
| [[Sprint Backlog]]  | Work selected for the Sprint plus a plan            |
| [[Increment]]       | Sum of completed work, meets [[Definition of Done]] |

## Scrum vs Other Frameworks

| Aspect | Scrum | [[Kanban]] | [[Extreme Programming\|XP]] |
|--------|-------|-----------|---------|
| **Focus** | Project management | Flow management | Engineering practices |
| **Iterations** | Fixed (Sprints) | Continuous | Short (1-2 weeks) |
| **Roles** | PO, SM, Dev Team | No prescribed roles | Whole team |
| **Prescribes** | Events, artifacts | Principles, practices | Engineering practices |

## When to Use Scrum

Scrum works well when:
- Requirements change frequently
- The problem is complex
- Creative solutions are needed
- Rapid feedback is valuable
- The team can self-organize

Scrum may be challenging when:
- Requirements are fixed and stable
- The team cannot self-organize
- The organization doesn't support empirical process control
